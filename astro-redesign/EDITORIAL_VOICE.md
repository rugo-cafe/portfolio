# editorial voice — notes for a personal website

version 0.1. This is a **working voice guide**, not a finished autobiography or an automatic style-transfer skill.

## intention
poetry by **precision, juxtaposition and silence**, not by adjectives. The reader should recognize a human with concrete interests: making interfaces, writing code, archiving radio broadcasts, experiments with sound and images.

## writing rules
1. start with an object, activity or decision: a note, a browser tab, a broadcast, a page, an archive, a sound layer.
2. prefer a short true sentence to a heroic or abstract claim.
3. keep the Portuguese natural, mostly lowercase, without excessive dashes, buzzwords or theatrical punctuation.
4. never invent emotional autobiography, achievements, metrics, names of collaborators, technology choices or motivations not supplied by the author.
5. avoid generic verbs when more precise actions are available: instead of “crio experiências”, use “misturo canais de áudio”, “mantenho um arquivo de transmissões”, “projeto telas”, “escrevo código”.
6. the poetic movement should arise from placing two concrete worlds together: radio + browser; privacy + blank page; image + dots.
7. no false mysticism: words such as “essência”, “jornada”, “narrativa”, “imersivo”, “refúgio”, “limites”, “experiências únicas” need justification or removal.
8. allow practical information to remain practical: portfolio visitors should still understand what each project does and how to open it.

## three layers of copy
- **naming:** functional, immediate labels (projetos, contato, abrir site).
- **description:** factual with one characteristic detail.
- **marginal notes:** optional poetic fragments, visually restrained, never necessary for navigation.

## what this is not
- not a preset “poetic” tone applied to every section.
- not imitation of a known writer or artist.
- not license to fill space with decorative text.
- not a substitute for the author's own editing.

## workflow for future revisions
For each passage, write two alternative versions: a literal one and a slightly elliptical one. Mark verifiable facts separately. Keep only the version that reads like something the author would actually say.
